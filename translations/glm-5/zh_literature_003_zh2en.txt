Grocery Zhang took Zhu Xingyun’s sudden disappearance in stride. She figured he wouldn’t die; in these times, men suddenly going missing was a common occurrence. At first, she was anxious for a spell, asking around everywhere for news, and even made a special trip to the countryside where her husband had gone. After coming up empty-handed, she stopped wasting her energy on it. She figured her husband must have his reasons for not coming home, otherwise he would have returned by now. It was useless to search the whole world for him. The burden of the family’s livelihood falling on her shoulders alone was already enough to make her stagger, but unexpectedly, Wang Jintang’s old wife came knocking at her door, insisting that Zhu Xingyun produce the man. She claimed that since Zhu Xingyun had taken Wang Jintang away, he should be the one to bring him back. Grocery Zhang would have none of it. She gave the old lady a piece of her mind and drove her out the door. Who knew that after this, the old lady would come to the grocery store every day. She didn't enter, but stood shivering in the cold wind, telling anyone who passed by, "Did you know? My hunchback went with Zhu Xingyun to pull the sticky bean bun cart for the grocery store, and to this day he hasn't come back. I came to ask them for my man, and this woman even cursed at me. Do you think she's being reasonable? What right does she have to curse at people the moment she opens her mouth?" At first, Grocery Zhang sent her two children out to chase her away, but seeing that they couldn't get rid of her at all, she took matters into her own hands, brandishing a fire poker and threatening to whack her over the head. The old lady had seen the world and wasn't intimidated at all. Grocery Zhang also felt that the old lady had become destitute because of her own family's affairs, so she might as well let her stay, claiming, "Just consider it as me picking up an old dog." Grocery Zhang even confidently pushed a wheelbarrow to Wang Jintang’s house and moved over every usable item one by one. She assured the old lady that the house was empty now and the things would be stolen by thieves; keeping them in the grocery store was just for safekeeping, and they would be returned intact once Wang Jintang came back. The old lady thought this made sense, so she let her do it. Grocery Zhang was quite shrewd; she quietly sold off the old lady’s belongings, thinking, "I can't just feed you for nothing. If your hunchback doesn't come back for eight or ten years, am I supposed to wait on you like this the whole time?" In her mind, this muddled, shaky old lady wouldn't live much longer, but who knew that over the past two years she would cling to life so tenaciously. She was always heard complaining about dizziness and weakness, yet she never fell once when walking alone. Although she didn't eat much, she never missed a meal, and the chopsticks in her hand never dropped once. Grocery Zhang snapped at her more than once: "You're really something, aren't you? Tough as nails. Aiming to live to a hundred, are you?" The old lady said dismissively, "What's a hundred years? Under the Emperor's very nose, I'm going to live to two hundred, three hundred!" Finally, she softened her tone and said listlessly, "I've lived enough; there's no point anymore. My son is gone, and my grandson is gone. My daughter married and ignores me; she hasn't come back to see me in years. I originally thought the hunchback would treat me well, but who knew he'd be just as heartless, slipping away on his own and leaving me all by myself. I can't swallow this. If he doesn't come back, I won't die. I have to ask him to his face why he said he'd stop caring for me and then just did. Even if I die, I must die knowing why!"
