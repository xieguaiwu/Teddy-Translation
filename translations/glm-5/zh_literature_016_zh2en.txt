Amidst the ding-dong sound of Zijing chopping the filling, Huangmao leaned his exhausted body against the wall and began telling the old woman tales from all over. "Blind Mother, have you ever heard this? A woman in Wanggezhuang got up early to fetch water, and suddenly saw a red lotus flower as big as a prayer cushion in the well. The red lotus was holding a fair, plump baby. The woman was bewitched, plunged headfirst into the well, and drowned—" "The lotus baby was a soul-snatching ghost in disguise," the old woman said. "—One day, it rained heavily, and eight masons ran into a ruined temple to take shelter. The thunder and lightning merged into a sheet, formed an egg, and fireballs rolled back and forth in front of the temple door. The people inside were scared out of their wits. One of them said, 'Among the eight of us, someone must have done something against their conscience. We can't let one rat dropping ruin a whole pot of porridge. Whoever is guilty must go out.' But who was willing to go? So they pushed and shoved each other, becoming a tangled, chaotic mess. Another person said, 'How about this: everyone take off your bamboo hats and throw them out the temple door. Whoever's hat is blown away by the wind, that person goes out to die.' One man bravely pulled open the temple door, and the wind and rain whooshed in. Everyone took turns throwing their hats out, but every single one was blown back. Seven were thrown, and all seven blown back. Only one person was left. He tremblingly picked up his hat and threw it out, and a gust of evil wind swept the hat away. The seven men said, 'It's you, go on out.' How could he be willing? The seven men didn't give him a choice; they lifted him up and threw him out—" "What happened then? Was he struck dead?" "—Blind Mother, listen to me. After that man was thrown out, he knelt on the ground. He kowtowed like pounding garlic, praying, 'Heavens, oh heavens, you cannot wrong a good person!' Just as he was praying, he heard a loud crash behind him. The entire ruined temple collapsed, the four walls caving inward, the roof pressing down. Not one of the seven men escaped; they were wrapped into a giant human-filled dumpling." "Oh my, such a thing could happen!" the old woman exclaimed repeatedly. The displeasure brought on by the gloomy weather completely vanished. Just as she was listening with great relish to Huangmao telling the next story, Zijing brought out the steaming dumplings. The old lady, still wanting more, made Huangmao promise to continue telling her stories after the meal. Zijing brought over a bowl of jellyfish skin and a dish of century eggs, pouted at Huangmao, and said, "There's a bottle of wine in the back window hole. Have a couple of sips to relieve your fatigue." The old woman said, "Have a little, you've worked hard all day." Huangmao took the wine, bit off the cap, and took three big gulps. The alcohol hit him quickly, and his face flushed with the vibrant red of peach blossoms. Zijing snatched the bottle from his hand, gulped down a mouthful, and tears instantly filled her eyes. Huangmao's face floated in the curling white steam, like a phantom drifting near and far.

A few people had already crawled into the hole along the tunnel. Lao Zhang was the first. He crawled in front, holding the light above the water to illuminate the way. But he could not see anything, only that patch of murky black water. The water constantly slapped his face, hurting him. He held his head high to avoid submerging in the water, and his body was nearly trapped in the sludge. He used great effort to crawl forward a few steps. The water ahead rushed over and pushed him back a step. In a moment of carelessness, his hand slipped, and the lamp fell into the water and went out. His head plunged into the water, his vision went black, and a ringing sounded in his ears.

"How could I forget the past?" he said, struggling with a red face. "The most important thing is that you have the husband you chose for yourself, and I have my Zhijun."

"The style is the man"—this saying is not absolutely credible. Writing is sometimes expression and revelation; at other times, it is concealment and evasion. Behind tear-jerking words, there may not be an absence of hidden things. I myself have had such experiences; often, when I longed to express myself, I instead did a lot of concealing, and I knew in my heart that what was hidden might be even more important than what was expressed. Why is this? Why conceal when you know it in your heart? Why evade when you know it is important?

"They want to eat me, and you alone could do nothing about it; but why must you join them? Men who eat men are capable of anything; they will eat me, and they will eat you too; within a gang, they will even eat each other. But if you just turn a corner, if you change immediately, then everyone will be at peace. Even though it has always been this way, today we can make a special effort to be good, and say it cannot be done! Big brother, I believe you can say it; the day before yesterday, when the tenant farmers asked for a rent reduction, you said it couldn't be done."

Zijin's way of speaking made Feng Zicun feel very uncomfortable. He rarely spoke, and even when he occasionally said a word or two, he was evasive, as if deliberately making it impossible for people to guess his thoughts.
