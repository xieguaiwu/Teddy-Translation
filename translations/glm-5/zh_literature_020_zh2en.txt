Shazaohua, missing for years, returned from who knows where. Well-preserved, she looked to be only in her thirties. She came to the pagoda to see her mother, but her mother's reaction was entirely indifferent. In the days that followed, she embroiled Sima Liang in a very classical life-and-death romance. She produced a glass marble, claiming it was a token of affection Sima Liang had given her, and then a large mirror, claiming it was a token of affection she had given him. She declared that she had preserved her virginity for Sima Liang to this day. Sima Liang, staying in the presidential suite on the top floor of the Osmanthus Building, had returned heavy-hearted this time and was in no mood to rekindle an old flame with Shazaohua. Yet Shazaohua followed him as closely as a pesky shadow, annoying Sima Liang to the point where he grimaced, stomped his feet, jumped up and down, and roared like thunder: "My dear cousin, what exactly do you want? I offer you money and you refuse! I offer you clothes and you refuse! I offer you jewelry and you refuse! What is it you want?!" Sima Liang shook off Shazaohua's hand tugging at the hem of his jacket and, furious and helpless, plopped down onto the sofa. His raised foot kicked over a glass carafe with a slender neck and a bulbous belly; water spilled across the table, soaking the carpet, and a dozen crimson roses dangled haphazardly over the table's edge. Wearing a black dress as sheer as cicada wings, Shazaohua knelt clinging beside Sima Liang, her pitch-black eyes staring fixedly at his face, making it impossible for him not to look her in the eye. Her head was delicate, her neck slender and smooth, with only a few fine wrinkles. Sima Liang, experienced with women, knew that a woman's neck was a tree ring she could not conceal; the neck of a fifty-year-old woman, if it didn't resemble a bloated intestine, looked like a rotting log. It was rare to see such a smooth, firm neck on a woman in her fifties like Shazaohua's—he had no idea how she maintained it. Sima Liang's gaze traced down her neck to the two deep hollows of her collarbones, and to the faintly visible breasts beneath the dress. Viewed from any angle, she did not look like a woman in her fifties; she was a flower frozen for half a century. A bottle of osmanthus wine buried beneath a pomegranate tree for half a century. The ice-cold flower awaited plucking; the mellow, thick wine awaited guzzling. Sima Liang extended a finger and jabbed Shazaohua's bare knee. She let out a moan, a deep flush spreading across her face like a sunset glow. Like a fearless hero facing life and death, she threw herself into Sima Liang's arms; her entwining arms wrapped around his neck, and she pressed her feverish breasts tightly against his face, rubbing back and forth until Sima Liang's nose grew slick with oil and stinging tears leaked from his eyes. Shazaohua said, "Brother Maliang, I waited for you for thirty years." Sima Liang replied, "Zaohua, give me a break. Waiting for me for thirty years—what a massive crime you've laid at my doorstep." Shazaohua said, "I am a virgin." Sima Liang said, "A female thief, and a virgin at that! If you're a virgin, I'll jump from this building!" Shazaohua wept with grievance, muttering to herself. Her mutterings grew heated until she jumped up, gave a leap, and shed her dress at her feet like a snake shedding its skin. Lying flat on her back on the carpet, she shrieked: "Sima Liang, just try me! If I'm not a virgin, I'll jump from this building!"
