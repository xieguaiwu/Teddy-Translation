China's Space Station Welcomes Fifth Batch of Scientific Experiment Projects

The China Manned Space Engineering Office announced that the Tiangong space station has officially launched its fifth batch of scientific experiment projects. This batch includes a total of 32 projects, covering multiple fields such as space life sciences, microgravity physics, space materials science, and astronomical observation, with international cooperation projects accounting for 40%. Research teams from 17 countries will conduct experiments through China's space station. To date, the Tiangong space station has completed over 200 scientific experiments, yielding a number of research outcomes with international influence.

The international cooperation model of China's space station has received positive evaluation from the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs. Through various forms such as joint experiments, mutual astronaut visits, and data sharing, China's space station is becoming a new platform for global space research cooperation. The expandable design of the space station also provides a technological verification platform for future deep space exploration missions. Research and development of related technologies for the manned lunar landing mission are also advancing in parallel, with the goal of landing Chinese astronauts on the moon expected to be achieved within this decade.
