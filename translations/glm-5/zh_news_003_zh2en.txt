New Quantum Computer Surpasses 1,000 Qubits

A team led by Pan Jianwei at the University of Science and Technology of China announced the successful development of a superconducting quantum computer with over 1,000 qubits. The quantum computer has achieved computing speeds on specific tasks that are unmatched by classical supercomputers, reaching a new milestone in quantum error correction efficiency. The research team stated that this breakthrough makes it possible to apply quantum computers in practical scenarios such as financial modeling and drug molecular simulation. The relevant findings have been published in the journal *Nature*.

The research and development competition for quantum computers is accelerating globally. Countries and regions such as the United States, the European Union, and Japan have successively launched large-scale investment plans for quantum technology. China has made significant progress across multiple technological routes, including superconducting quantum computing, photonic quantum computing, and ion traps. Experts note that the commercial application of quantum computing still needs to overcome key technical challenges such as qubit coherence time, error correction efficiency, and system integration. However, breakthroughs in basic research are continuously narrowing the gap from the laboratory to industrialization.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these changes will have a profound impact on the landscape of industry development. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous interaction. In the coming years, with the deepening advancement of technological iteration and model innovation, related fields are expected to witness faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society hold high expectations for this.
