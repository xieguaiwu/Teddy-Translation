"Ci, you love the sheep; I love the ritual!"

This quote is from the "Ba Yi" chapter of *The Analects*: "Zigong wanted to do away with the sheep offered at the Gushuo ceremony. The Master said, 'Ci, you love the sheep; I love the ritual!'" According to the annotations by Zhu Xi of the Song Dynasty: the sheep offered is a live sheep. The feudal lords reporting to the ancestral temple and attending to government affairs on the first day of each month was called Gushuo. Zigong (Duanmu Ci), seeing that the ruler of the State of Lu had already abolished the Gushuo ritual, wanted to also do away with the sheep prepared for the ceremony; but Confucius believed that keeping the sheep could at least preserve some form of the ritual, hence his words.

(1) Ci originates from Yuefu, and Yuefu originates from Shi. The materials of the Major and Minor *Ya* among the four sections of the *Classic of Poetry* total one hundred and five. The elegant among these materials are what makes *Feng* beautiful and what forms *Song*. From Shi to Yuefu to Ci, one must aspire to the single word "elegance" in order to stand out and claim the title of a creator. Therefore, Zeng Duanbo named his selected ci *Yuefu Yaci* [Elegant Yuefu Ci], and Zhou Gongjin, being skilled at writing ci, named his hall "Zhiya" [Aspiration for Elegance].
