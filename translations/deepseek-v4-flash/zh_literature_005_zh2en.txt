I had worked in that kitchen for two months, but it felt like several years. Because it was endless washing dishes and dumping trash. Besides that, there was a sadistic second chef and a miserably harsh boss, Zhou Bapi, whom I always wanted to spit on. I only worked two nights a week, and those days felt like years, let alone Boss Li who stayed all day in the shop he once owned. He was prematurely aged, slow in movement; Zhou Bapi said taking him in was a good deed, so he couldn't pay him much. For these reasons, my fellow townsman came to talk with me again. After our shifts, we waited for the bus. In the dead of night, we waited one or two hours and the bus never came. He swore that the Italian girl had been sincere to him at first, but later changed. Eventually, she said she was leaving him but didn't want his money. Moreover, she found him a wife, a Peruvian woman. She couldn't be called white, black, or red, because South Americans have the most mixed blood. He didn't understand Spanish, she didn't understand Chinese, and the common American language, English, both were clueless about. There was one thing they could do without speaking, so they did it, and children came one after another. Each one was neither black nor yellow, simply grotesque. Another oddity: all those children spoke their mother's language, not a word of Chinese. When he came home, he was silently surrounded. That atmosphere was terrifying. Only by beating a few into crying could he feel better. He told me that looking at a house full of brats, he hardly knew what he had done.

“Ci, you love the sheep, but I love the ritual!” This comes from the Analects, Ba Yi: “Zigong wanted to do away with the live sheep for the monthly announcement ceremony. The Master said: ‘Ci, you love the sheep, but I love the ritual!’” According to Zhu Xi’s commentary from the Song Dynasty, the live sheep was for the ceremony. The feudal lords would go to the ancestral temple on the first day of each month to hear government affairs, called the monthly announcement. Zigong (Duanmu Ci) saw that the Duke of Lu had already abolished the ceremony and wanted to also remove the sheep prepared for the ritual; but Confucius thought that keeping the sheep would still preserve a form of the ritual, hence he said this.

(1) Ci poetry originates from yuefu, and yuefu originates from the Classic of Poetry. The four sections of the Odes, including the Major and Minor Hymns, contain one hundred and five pieces in total. The refined pieces among them are the source of the beauty of the Airs and the completion of the Eulogies. From the Classic of Poetry to yuefu to ci, one must attain the quality of refinement to stand out as an author. Hence Zeng Duanbo titled his selection of ci as ‘Elegant Ci of Yuefu,’ and Zhou Gongjin, skilled in ci, named his studio ‘Aspiration for Refinement.’ The form of ci is intricate and slow; if not interwoven with refinement, few would avoid drifting with the tide and losing their correctness. (From the preface to the Collection of Group Elegant Ci.)
