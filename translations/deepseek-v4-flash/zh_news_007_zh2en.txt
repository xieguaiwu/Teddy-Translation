Urban Renewal Initiatives Drive Renovation of Old Residential Communities

The latest report from the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development shows that a cumulative total of 280,000 old urban residential communities have undergone renovation nationwide, benefiting over 65 million households. In 2026, the plan is to renovate 53,000 such communities, with a focus on installing elevators, upgrading water, electricity, gas, and heating networks, and adding parking spaces and charging stations. Local governments are also exploring new models to involve social capital in renovation and operation. Experts point out that urban renewal is not only a livelihood project but also an important way to boost domestic demand and promote economic growth.

During the renovation process, local authorities are also exploring the establishment of long-term management mechanisms. After renovation, communities ensure the sustainability of the results by introducing professional property management, forming homeowners' committees, and setting up maintenance funds. The integration of smart community construction with old community renovation has promoted the adoption of smart access control, smart parking, and smart waste sorting facilities. Resident satisfaction surveys show that living comfort in renovated communities has increased by an average of over 40%.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts note that these changes will have a profound impact on the development landscape of the sector. In the long term, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with deeper technological advancements and model innovations, the relevant fields are expected to usher in faster and higher-quality development. Society is full of anticipation for this.
