The world's largest offshore wind power project has been connected to the grid and began generating electricity. Built by a Chinese company, the project achieved full-capacity grid connection in the waters off Yangjiang, Guangdong. With a total installed capacity of 6.5 GW, it generates approximately 26 billion kWh of electricity annually, enough to meet the yearly power needs of about 9 million households. The project utilizes multiple domestically produced large-capacity wind turbines, reducing construction costs. As a key component of clean energy, offshore wind power is becoming a crucial pillar in China's energy structure transformation.

The construction of offshore wind power projects has also driven the export of related equipment manufacturing and engineering technical services. Chinese-made wind turbines and installation vessels have been exported to Europe, Southeast Asia, and other countries and regions. Integrated development models combining offshore wind power with marine ranching, seawater hydrogen production, and wave energy generation are also being explored. Breakthroughs in deep-sea floating wind power technology have laid the foundation for developing wind energy resources in deeper and more distant waters. The rapid development of the offshore wind power industry provides significant support for China's goals of achieving carbon peak and carbon neutrality.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that related changes will have a profound impact on the development pattern of the industry. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with the deepening of technological iteration and model innovation, related fields are expected to experience faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society are looking forward to this.
