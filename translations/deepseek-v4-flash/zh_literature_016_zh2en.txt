Amidst the rhythmic chopping of bauhinia leaves as Zijing made dumpling filling, Yellow Hair leaned his weary body against the wall and began to regale the old woman with tales from near and far. "Blind mother, have you ever heard that in Wangge Village, a woman went to draw water at dawn and suddenly saw a lotus as large as a cattail cushion in the well, with a plump white baby boy cradled on it. She was bewitched, lost her senses, and plunged in, drowning herself—" "That lotus baby is a shape-shifting demon of death," the old woman said. "One day during a heavy rain, eight bricklayers took shelter in a dilapidated temple. The thunder and lightning became a continuous mass of balls of fire rolling at the temple gate, and everyone inside was terrified. One of them said, 'Among us eight, one must have done something wicked. We can't let one bad apple spoil the whole barrel. Whoever is guilty should go out.' But who would admit to it? They pushed and shoved, tangled in confusion. Another said, 'Let's all take off our straw hats and throw them out the temple gate. Whoever's hat is blown away by the wind must go out and face death.' One man bravely pulled the door open, and wind and rain burst in. They took turns throwing their hats out. One after another, each was blown back, until seven hats had all returned. Only one man was left. Trembling, he picked up his hat and threw it out. A sinister wind caught it and swept it away. The seven said, 'It's you. Go out.' How could he agree? Without further ado, they lifted him and threw him out—" "And then? Was he struck dead?" "Blind mother, listen. The man thrown out knelt on the ground, kowtowing like pounding garlic, praying, 'Heaven, oh Heaven, you cannot wrong an innocent man!' While he prayed, he heard a loud crash behind him. The whole dilapidated temple collapsed, walls falling inward, roof pressing down. Not one of the seven escaped; they were wrapped up like a big dumpling of human flesh—" "Oh my! Such a thing could happen!" The old woman sighed repeatedly. The gloom of the overcast day had entirely vanished. Just as she was eagerly listening to Yellow Hair's next story, Zijing brought up steaming hot dumplings. The old woman, still in good spirits, told Yellow Hair to continue telling stories after the meal. Zijing set down a bowl of jellyfish skin and a plate of preserved eggs, pouted at Yellow Hair, and said, "There's a bottle of wine in the back window alcove. Have a couple of sips to ease your fatigue." The old woman added, "Have a drink, you've worked hard all day." Yellow Hair took the wine, bit off the cap, and took three big gulps. The alcohol quickly took effect, and his face flushed with a peach-blossom red. Zijing snatched the bottle from his hand and gulped down a mouthful, tears instantly welling in her eyes. Yellow Hair's face floated in the swirling white steam, now near, now far, like a phantom.

Several people had already crawled along the tunnel into the opening. Old Zhang was first. He crawled ahead, holding the lamp above the water to light the way. But he could see nothing except the dark, murky water. The water kept hitting his face, stinging. He lifted his head to avoid submerging, but his body was nearly trapped in the mud. He mustered great effort to crawl forward a few steps. The oncoming water pushed him back a step. Carelessly, he let go, and the lamp fell into the water and went out. His head dipped into the water, darkness before his eyes, bells ringing in his ears.

"How can I forget what happened before?" he said, struggling, his face red. "The important thing is that you have the husband you chose yourself, and I have my Zhijun."

Behind tear-jerking words, there may not necessarily be nothing hidden. I myself have had this experience: often when I longed to express something, I did much concealing, and I knew in my heart that what was hidden might be more important than what was expressed. Why is this? Why hide when you know it in your heart? Why avoid when you know it's important?

"They want to eat me. Alone, you could do nothing; but why join them? People who eat human flesh will do anything; they'll eat me, they'll eat you, and among themselves, they'll eat each other. But if they take one step back, if they change immediately, everyone can be at peace. Though it has always been this way, we could still be especially good today. To say it's impossible! Zijin's way of speaking made Feng Zicun very uncomfortable. He rarely spoke, and even when he occasionally uttered a word or two, he was evasive, as if deliberately making it hard for others to guess his thoughts.
