The long-missing Sha Zao Hua returned from somewhere unknown, well-preserved and looking to be in her early thirties. She came to the pagoda to visit her mother, who responded with indifference. In the days that followed, she staged a very classical life-and-death romance with Sima Liang. She produced a glass ball, claiming it was the token of love Sima Liang had given her. Then she brought out a large mirror, saying it was her gift to him. She asserted that she had kept her chastity for Sima Liang all these years. Sima Liang, staying in the presidential suite on the top floor of the Osmanthus Building, had returned with a heavy heart and no desire to rekindle old feelings with Sha Zao Hua. Yet Sha Zao Hua clung to him like a shadow, driving Sima Liang to grit his teeth, stomp and jump, and roar like thunder: "My dear cousin, what exactly do you want? I offer you money, but you don't take it; I offer you clothes, but you don't take them; I offer you jewelry, but you don't take it—what do you want?!" Sima Liang shook off Sha Zao Hua's hand clutching his coat, angrily and helplessly plopped down onto the sofa, and his crossed foot kicked over a slender-necked, big-bellied glass bottle. Water spilled across the table, soaking the carpet, and a dozen or so purple-red roses hung messily over the table's edge. Sha Zao Hua, wearing a black dress as thin as cicada wings, knelt clingingly beside Sima Liang, her jet-black eyes fixed on his face, forcing him to look at her. Her head was delicate, her neck slender and smooth, with only a few fine wrinkles. Sima Liang, experienced with women, knew that a woman's neck is an un-hideable ring of age—a fifty-year-old woman's neck is either like a bloated segment of large intestine or a piece of decayed deadwood. It was rare for a woman over fifty to have such a smooth and upright neck as Sha Zao Hua's; he wondered how she maintained it. His gaze traveled down her neck to her deeply sunken collarbone sockets and the vague outline of her breasts beneath the dress. From any angle, she didn't look like a woman over fifty; she was a flower refrigerated for half a century. A bottle of osmanthus wine buried under a pomegranate tree for half a century. A chilled flower awaiting plucking, a viscous wine awaiting hearty drinking. Sima Liang stretched out a finger and poked Sha Zao Hua's bare knee. She moaned, her face flushing with color like a sunset glow. Like a hero undaunted by life or death, she suddenly threw herself into Sima Liang's arms, her lingering arms wrapping around his neck, her hot chest pressing tightly against his face, rubbing and kneading until Sima Liang's nose was greasy and sour tears flowed from his eyes. Sha Zao Hua said, "Brother Liang, I've waited for you for thirty years." Sima Liang said, "Zao Hua, spare me that. Waiting for me for thirty years—what a burden you're putting on my head." Sha Zao Hua said, "I am a virgin." Sima Liang said, "A female thief, and a virgin? If you're a virgin, I'll jump off this building!" Sha Zao Hua cried in grievance, mumbling to herself, then her muttering turned into anger, and she jumped up, hopping, and let her dress fall to her feet like a snake shedding its skin. Lying face-up on the carpet, she shouted, "Sima Liang, go ahead and try it! If I'm not a virgin, I'll jump off the building!"
