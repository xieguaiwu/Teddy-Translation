New Curriculum Standards for Compulsory Education Fully Implemented

The Ministry of Education announced that starting from the fall semester of 2026, the new version of the compulsory education curriculum standards will be fully implemented in primary and secondary schools nationwide. The new standards focus on core competency orientation, strengthening the proportion of science education, labor education, and arts education. Among the changes, science classes will begin in first grade instead of third grade, and physical education and health class hours will increase to 10% to 11% of total instruction time. Textbook compilation and teacher training efforts are already fully underway, covering an estimated 160 million primary and secondary school students.

The advancement of compulsory education reform also faces challenges such as balanced regional development and the construction of a qualified teaching workforce. School conditions in central and western regions as well as rural areas are continuously improving. Policies such as the Special Post Plan and the Silver Age Teaching Plan have been implemented to supplement teachers in shortage subjects. The National Education Digital Strategy Action has promoted the co-construction and sharing of high-quality educational resources. The continued implementation of the "double reduction" policy has eased students' academic burden and off-campus tutoring pressure. Education evaluation reform is also exploring the establishment of a more scientific and diverse evaluation system.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these changes will have a profound impact on the development landscape of the sector. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and collaboration to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. Continuous policy optimization and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with the deepening of technological iteration and model innovation, related fields are expected to experience faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society are looking forward to this with great anticipation.
