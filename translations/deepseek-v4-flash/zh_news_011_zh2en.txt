China's Forest Coverage Rate Rises to 25.3%

The latest data released by the National Forestry and Grassland Administration shows that the national forest coverage rate has increased to 25.3%, with a forest area of 245 million hectares. Over the past decade, China has added more than 60 million hectares of forest, making it the country with the largest growth in forest resources globally. Projects such as the Three-North Shelterbelt Forest Program, the Grain for Green Program, and nationwide land greening initiatives have been steadily advancing. International environmental organizations point out that China's afforestation efforts have made substantial contributions to global carbon emission reduction and biodiversity conservation.

The value of forest ecosystem services is also continuously increasing. The forest carbon sink trading market is gradually improving, with forestry carbon sink projects providing new income channels for people in forested areas. The pilot program for the national park system is being deepened, and a system of protected areas centered on national parks is taking shape. A comprehensive system for the protection and restoration of natural forests has been established, with responsibilities for forest resource management assigned to specific individuals. Green industries such as under-forest economy, forest tourism, and forest health care are flourishing, achieving a win-win situation for both ecological protection and economic development.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that related changes will have a profound impact on the development landscape of the industry. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a beneficial interaction. In the coming years, with the deepening of technological iteration and model innovation, relevant fields are expected to experience faster and higher-quality development. Society at large is full of expectations for this.
