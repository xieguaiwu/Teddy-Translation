Grocer Zhang took Zhu Xingyun's sudden disappearance in stride. She figured he wasn't dead—men vanishing without a trace was common enough in these times. At first she was frantic, asking around everywhere, even making a special trip to the countryside where her husband had gone. When that turned up nothing, she stopped wasting her energy. She figured her husband must have had a reason not to come home, otherwise he would have returned long ago. Searching the whole world for him was pointless. She was already staggering under the weight of supporting the family alone when Wang Jintang's wife showed up at her door, insisting that Zhu Xingyun produce the man—since he had taken Wang Jintang away, he should bring him back. Grocer Zhang wasn't having any of it. She cursed the old woman out and chased her from the door. But that wasn't the end of it. From then on, the old woman came to the grocery every day. She wouldn't come inside, just stood shivering in the cold wind, telling everyone who passed, "Did you know? My hunchback went with Zhu Xingyun to haul sticky rice cakes for the grocery, and he hasn't come back yet. I come here to demand him, and that woman curses me out. Is that fair or not? What right does she have to curse people?" At first, Grocer Zhang sent her children out to drive her away, but when that didn't work, she came out herself, brandishing a fire poker and threatening to crack the old woman's skull. The old woman had seen plenty in her time and wasn't intimidated. Grocer Zhang also felt that the old woman had become lonely and wretched because of her own family's troubles, so she finally let her stay, saying, "Might as well say I've picked up an old dog." Grocer Zhang even had the nerve to push her wheelbarrow over to Wang Jintang's house and cart back whatever was useful, assuring the old woman, "That house is empty now; things inside will get stolen. I'm just storing them in my shop for safekeeping. When Wang Jintang comes back, I'll return everything intact." The old woman thought this reasonable and let her be. But Grocer Zhang was shrewd. She quietly sold off the old woman's belongings, thinking, I can't support you for nothing. If your hunchback doesn't come back for ten years, am I supposed to wait on you forever? In her view, this muddle-headed, tottering old woman wouldn't last much longer. But to her surprise, the old woman clung stubbornly to life over the next two years. She was always complaining of dizziness and weakness, yet she never once fell while walking alone. Though her appetite was small, she never missed a meal, and the chopsticks never slipped from her hand. Grocer Zhang often snapped at her: "You're something, aren't you? You can endure, you can live—aiming for a hundred, are you?" The old woman replied nonchalantly, "What's a hundred years? Right under the emperor's nose, I'll live to two hundred, three hundred!" Then she softened her voice, weary and listless: "I've lived long enough—it's no fun anymore. My son's gone, my grandson's gone. My daughter married and abandoned me, hasn't come back to see me in years. I thought the hunchback would take care of me, but he's heartless too—slipped away on his own, leaving me behind. I can't swallow this anger. If he doesn't come back, I won't die. I have to see him and ask why he just abandoned me like that. I need to die knowing why!"
