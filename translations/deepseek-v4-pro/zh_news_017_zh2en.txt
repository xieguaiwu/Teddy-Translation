China's cross-border e-commerce trade volume hits a new record high. Data released by the Ministry of Commerce shows that in 2025, China's cross-border e-commerce import and export volume reached 2.8 trillion yuan, a year-on-year increase of 32%. Exports are mainly clothing, footwear, electronics, and household goods, while imports are dominated by beauty and skincare products, maternal and infant products, and food and health supplements. Growth has been particularly notable in emerging markets such as Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America. The number of overseas warehouses has exceeded 2,500, covering major consumer markets around the world. Cross-border e-commerce is becoming an important new engine for foreign trade growth.

The rapid development of cross-border e-commerce has also benefited from continuous improvements in logistics infrastructure and the international payment system. Companies such as Cainiao Network and JD Logistics have established efficient sorting and distribution networks overseas, reducing cross-border parcel delivery times from two weeks to three to five days. Cross-border payment platforms support multi-currency settlement and localized payment methods. The number of comprehensive cross-border e-commerce pilot zones has been expanded to 165, yielding a range of replicable and scalable institutional innovations in customs clearance, taxation, and settlement.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts note that these changes will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long term, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. Continuous policy optimization and positive market feedback have created a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, as technological iteration and model innovation advance further, the relevant fields are expected to achieve even faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society are full of expectations for this.
