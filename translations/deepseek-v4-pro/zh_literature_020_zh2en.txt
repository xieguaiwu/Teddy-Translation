Shazao Hua, who had been missing for many years, returned from nowhere. She had taken good care of herself and looked only in her thirties. She came to the pagoda to see her mother, whose reaction was very indifferent. In the days that followed, she engaged in a very classical life-and-death romance with Sima Liang. She took out a glass ball and said it was a token of love Sima Liang had given her. She also took out a large mirror and said it was the token she had given him. She claimed she had preserved her virginity for Sima Liang to this day. Sima Liang, who was staying in the presidential suite on the top floor of the Osmanthus Building, had returned with a heavy heart and was in no mood to rekindle his old romance with Shazao Hua. But Shazao Hua tagged along like a shadow, so annoying that Sima Liang ground his teeth, stamped his feet, and roared like thunder: “My dear cousin, what on earth do you want? I give you money and you won’t take it, I give you clothes and you won’t take them, I give you jewelry and you won’t take it. What do you want?!” Sima Liang shook off Shazao Hua’s hand clutching the hem of his coat, angrily and helplessly plopped himself down on the sofa. His raised foot knocked over a glass water jug with a slender neck and a big belly, water spilled all over the table, soaking the carpet, and over a dozen purple-red roses dangled messily over the edge of the table. Shazao Hua wore a black dress as thin as cicada wings, kneeling clingingly beside Sima Liang, her pitch-black eyes staring straight into his face, forcing him to look at her. Her head was delicate, her neck slender and smooth, with only a few fine wrinkles. Sima Liang, experienced with women, knew that a woman’s neck is the growth ring she cannot hide. A fifty-year-old woman’s neck, if not like a bloated section of intestine, would be like a rotten piece of dead wood. It was rare to see a neck as smooth and upright as Shazao Hua’s at over fifty. He wondered how she maintained it. Sima Liang looked down along her neck and saw her two deep-set shoulder hollows, and the hazy outline of her breasts under the dress. No matter which part he looked at, she did not look like a woman over fifty. She was a flower that had been kept refrigerated for half a century. A bottle of osmanthus wine buried under a pomegranate tree for half a century. The ice-cold flower waits to be plucked; the thick, sticky wine waits to be drunk. Sima Liang stretched out a finger and poked Shazao Hua’s bare knee. She let out a moan, her face flushed with blood like a patch of sunset clouds. Like a hero who fears neither life nor death, she suddenly threw herself into Sima Liang’s arms, her lingering arms wrapped around his neck, her warm chest pressed against his face, rubbing and kneading until Sima Liang’s nose oozed oil and his eyes shed sour tears. Shazao Hua said, “Brother Ma Liang, I’ve waited for you for thirty years.” Sima Liang said, “Zao Hua, don’t give me that. Waiting for me for thirty years? What a big crime you’re pinning on me.” Shazao Hua said, “I am a virgin.” Sima Liang said, “A female thief, and a virgin at that? If you’re a virgin, I’ll jump off this building!” Shazao Hua wept aggrievedly, muttering under her breath, her muttering turning to fire; she jumped up, gave a bounce, shed her dress like a snake sloughing its skin, and lay face-up on the carpet shouting, “Sima Liang, try and see! If I’m not a virgin, I’ll jump off the building!”
