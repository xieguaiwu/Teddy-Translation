Glacier Melt Accelerates in Everest Region

The latest monitoring report released by the Institute of Tibetan Plateau Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, shows that glaciers in the Mount Everest region have melted about 2.5 times faster over the past decade than in the previous 30 years. Using satellite remote sensing data and ground observation station records, researchers found that the total mass loss of glaciers in the Everest region reached a record 1.2 billion tonnes in 2025. Global warming is the main cause of the accelerated glacier melt. This trend will affect water supply for several major rivers in Asia, impacting hundreds of millions of people.

The accelerating glacier melt has already had a direct impact on surrounding ecosystems and the lives of residents. Lakes formed by glacial meltwater are expanding, increasing the risk of glacial lake outburst floods. Downstream river runoff may increase in the short term but will face reduction threats in the long term. Researchers call for strengthening the glacier monitoring network and developing regional water resource management plans to adapt to climate change. The glacier changes in the Everest region also serve as a stark warning signal for global warming.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that the relevant changes will have a profound impact on the development landscape of the sector.
