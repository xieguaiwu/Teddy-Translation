Grocer Zhang took Zhu Xingyun's sudden disappearance in stride, figuring he wouldn't die—men vanishing without a trace in these times was all too common. At first, she did fret for a while, asking around everywhere and even traveling to the countryside her husband had gone to, but after finding nothing, she stopped wearing herself out over it. She reasoned that he must be unable to come home for some reason; otherwise, he would have returned long ago. Searching the whole world for him was useless. The family’s burden of making a living had already fallen squarely on her shoulders, leaving her stumbling and struggling, when unexpectedly Wang Jintang’s old wife showed up at her door, insisting that Zhu Xingyun produce the man, claiming he had taken Wang Jintang away and should be the one to bring him back. Grocer Zhang wasn’t having any of it, cursed the old woman out, and drove her from the door. Who could have guessed that after that, the old woman would come to the grocery shop every single day? She wouldn’t enter, just stood shivering in the cold wind, telling anyone she met, "Do you know? My hunchback went off with Zhu Xingyun to haul sticky bean buns for the grocery shop, and the man still hasn’t come back. I came to ask them for him, and this woman cursed me—tell me, is she being reasonable or not? What gives her the right to open her mouth and curse people?" At first, Grocer Zhang sent her pair of children out to chase her away, but seeing they couldn’t budge her at all, she took matters into her own hands, brandishing a fire poker and threatening to crack her over the head. The old woman had seen the world and wasn’t falling for that. Grocer Zhang also felt the old woman had ended up alone and pitiable because of her own family’s trouble, so she simply let her stay, declaring, "I’ll just treat it as if I’ve picked up an old dog." Grocer Zhang even righteously wheeled a handcart over to Wang Jintang’s house and carted away everything usable, one item after another, assuring the old woman that the house was now empty and the belongings left inside would be stolen by thieves; storing them at the grocery shop was just safekeeping, and when Wang Jintang returned, everything would be returned intact. The old woman thought it made sense, so she let her be. Grocer Zhang, quite calculating, secretly sold off the old woman’s things, thinking, I can’t support you for nothing—if your hunchback doesn’t come back for eight or ten years, am I supposed to keep waiting on you like this forever? In her view, this muddle-headed, trembling old woman couldn’t live much longer anyway, but who would have thought that these past two years she’d prove so stubbornly resilient? She was always heard complaining of dizziness and weakness, yet not once did she fall when walking alone. Her appetite wasn’t large, but she never missed a meal, and her chopsticks never slipped from her hand. Grocer Zhang more than once rebuked her: "You’re doing well, enduring and living on—planning to aim for a hundred years old?" The old woman, unfazed, said, "What’s a hundred years? Under the emperor’s very nose, I’ve got to live to two hundred, three hundred!" Then, lowering her voice, weak and listless, she added, "I’ve lived enough, there’s no point anymore. My son’s gone, my grandson’s gone. My daughter married off and ignores me now, hasn’t come back to see me once in years. I thought the hunchback would treat me well, but who knew he’d be heartless too, sneaking off on his own, leaving me alone? I can’t swallow this grievance. If he doesn’t come back, I won’t die—I need to see him and ask him why he just abandoned me without a word. Even if I die, I’ve got to die understanding why!"
