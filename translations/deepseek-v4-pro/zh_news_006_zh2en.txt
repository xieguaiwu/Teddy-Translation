China Space Station Welcomes Fifth Batch of Science Experiments

The China Manned Space Engineering Office announced that the Tiangong space station has officially launched its fifth batch of science experiment projects. This batch includes a total of 32 projects, covering space life sciences, microgravity physics, space materials science, astronomical observation, and other fields, with international cooperation projects accounting for 40%. Research teams from 17 countries will conduct experiments aboard the Chinese space station. To date, Tiangong has completed over 200 scientific experiments and produced a number of research results with international influence.

China's model of international cooperation on the space station has received positive evaluation from the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs. Through joint experiments, astronaut exchanges, data sharing, and other forms, the Chinese space station is becoming a new platform for global space research cooperation. The station's scalable design also provides a technology verification platform for future deep-space exploration missions. Research and development of technologies related to crewed lunar landing missions are also advancing simultaneously, with the goal of landing Chinese astronauts on the moon within this decade.
