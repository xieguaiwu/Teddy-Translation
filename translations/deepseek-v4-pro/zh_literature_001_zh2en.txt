Beyond this, dealing with the higher offices has become more and more difficult. In the days that followed, Sundries Zhang came nearly every day to the school to make a row. Zhu Mei trumpeted grandiosely among her schoolmates how her younger brother, to support the Great East Asia War, had not hesitated to smash and break one of his own legs. Those who heard could do nothing but cover their faces and laugh. Seeing that the old lady’s arm was not in the least diminished by the torment of the diet, Zhu Mei abandoned any thought of that white jade bracelet. On reflection, to truly cut off her arm — she still could not bring herself to do it. Only thereafter she treated the old lady with even greater insolence and rudeness. Zhu Yan’s wound was slow to heal. Finally, one afternoon, Sundries Zhang managed to secure some benefits for Zhu Yan: the school paid a part of the cost for treating his leg, and the person in charge of logistics bought him several jin of beef bones. Sundries Zhang, in high spirits, brought them home and simmered the beef bones. Before she had even ladled a bowl of the fragrant, yellow-oil-globuled beef-bone soup for Zhu Yan, she first served herself a bowl and noisily drank it up. As she drank, she thought she must finish off this whole pot of bones before Zhu Mei came home, leaving her not a shred of meat, not a drop of broth. In the past she had resented the old lady’s eating, but this time she egged her on heartily. The result was that when Zhu Mei returned home, the grocery shop was steeped in a tantalizing aroma of meat, but the pot held nothing else. Zhu Mei squinted at the bare, glistening bones gnawed clean beside the stove and ground her teeth, cursing: "You three white-eyed wolves, you three turtle’s eggs!" None of the three had the strength to reckon with her; they were truly too exhausted from eating. Zhu Mei snatched up a bone and hurled it at the mirror on the wall.

Laidi had followed Birdman Han to catch birds. Birdman Han, whose allure had long since faded, had returned to his old trade. In the daylight he vowed to catch a few herons for Laidi to nourish her body. They walked along the paths between the fields; the air was crisp and cold, and the two clung tightly to one another. Birdman Han’s dauntless, heaven-and-earth-defying air infected Laidi, temporarily lifting from her the heavy burden of her spirit. The bird-like scent emanating from his armpits brought her a forlorn warmth. She murmured, "Birdman Han, Birdman Han, the mute will find out sooner or later, he will never spare us…" Birdman Han tightened his arm around her waist, piping a string of fascinating, clarion whistles from his lips.
