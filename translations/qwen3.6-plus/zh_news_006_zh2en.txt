China's Space Station Welcomes Fifth Batch of Scientific Experiment Projects

The China Manned Space Agency has announced that the Tiangong space station has officially launched its fifth batch of scientific experiment projects. This batch comprises 32 projects spanning multiple fields, including space life sciences, microgravity physics, space materials science, and astronomical observation, with international cooperation projects accounting for 40%. Research teams from 17 countries will conduct experiments aboard the Chinese space station. To date, the Tiangong space station has successfully completed over 200 scientific experiments, yielding a series of research outcomes with international impact.

The international cooperation model of China's space station has received positive recognition from the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs. Through joint experiments, astronaut exchanges, and data sharing, the Tiangong space station is emerging as a new global platform for space research collaboration. Its scalable design also provides a technological verification platform for future deep space exploration missions. Concurrently, research and development for crewed lunar missions are advancing steadily, with the goal of landing Chinese astronauts on the moon expected to be achieved within this decade.
