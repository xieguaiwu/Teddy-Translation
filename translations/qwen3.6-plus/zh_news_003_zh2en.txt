New Quantum Computer Surpasses 1,000 Qubits

A team led by Pan Jianwei at the University of Science and Technology of China has announced the successful development of a superconducting quantum computer with over 1,000 qubits. For specific computational tasks, the machine achieves speeds unmatched by classical supercomputers, while its quantum error correction efficiency has reached a new milestone. The research team stated that this breakthrough paves the way for applying quantum computers in practical scenarios such as financial modeling and drug molecule simulation. The related findings have been published in the journal *Nature*.

The global race to develop quantum computers is accelerating. Countries and regions including the United States, the European Union, and Japan have successively rolled out large-scale investment plans in quantum technology. China has also made significant progress across multiple technical pathways, including superconducting quantum computing, photonic quantum computing, and ion traps. Experts note that while the commercial application of quantum computing still requires overcoming key technical challenges such as qubit coherence time, error correction efficiency, and system integration, breakthroughs in basic research are steadily narrowing the gap between laboratory experiments and industrialization.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these developments will profoundly reshape the industry landscape. In the long run, all stakeholders need to enhance collaboration to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. Continuous policy optimization and positive market feedback have fostered a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, as technological iteration and model innovation advance, the sector is expected to see faster and higher-quality development. Society at large holds high expectations for these advancements.
