Grocer Zhang took Zhu Xingyun’s sudden disappearance in stride. She figured he wasn’t dead; in these times, it was common enough for men to vanish without a trace. At first, she had been frantic, asking everyone she knew to help look for him, and even made a special trip to the countryside where he had gone. Finding nothing, she stopped wasting her energy on it. She reasoned that he must have found himself unable to return home, otherwise he would have been back by now. Searching the whole world for him would be pointless. The heavy burden of supporting the family had already fallen entirely on her shoulders, leaving her staggering under the weight. To make matters worse, Wang Jintang’s wife came knocking, demanding that Zhu Xingyun hand over her husband. She claimed Zhu had taken Wang Jintang away to haul sticky bean buns for the grocery, so it was Zhu’s responsibility to bring him back. Grocer Zhang wouldn’t have any of it. After cursing the old woman out, she drove her out the door. But the old woman returned every day after that. She wouldn’t come inside, just stood shivering in the biting wind, telling anyone who passed by, “Did you know? My hunchback went with Zhu Xingyun to pull sticky bean buns for the grocery, and he still hasn’t come back. I come here asking for him, and this woman curses me out. Tell me, is that reasonable? What gives her the right to just start swearing at people?” At first, Grocer Zhang sent her son and daughter out to chase her away. When that proved useless, she took matters into her own hands, brandishing a fire poker and threatening to crack it over the old woman’s head. But the old woman had seen her share of the world and wasn’t intimidated. Grocer Zhang eventually felt that the woman had been left destitute and alone because of her own family’s affairs, so she simply let her stay, declaring, “Consider it as if I’ve picked up an old stray dog.” Grocer Zhang also self-righteously pushed a wheelbarrow to Wang Jintang’s house and moved every usable item over, one by one. She assured the old woman that since the house was now empty, the things inside would be stolen by thieves, so they were merely being stored at the grocery and would be returned intact once Wang Jintang came back. The old woman found this reasonable and let it be. Grocer Zhang was quite calculating, though. She quietly sold off the old woman’s belongings, thinking, *I can’t feed you for nothing. If your hunchback doesn’t come back for ten or twenty years, am I supposed to keep waiting on you like this forever?* In her mind, this confused, frail old woman wouldn’t last long anyway. Yet, over the past two years, the woman had proven remarkably resilient. She was always complaining of dizziness and weakness, but she had never once fallen while walking alone. Though her appetite wasn’t large, she never missed a meal, and her chopsticks never slipped from her hand. More than once, Grocer Zhang snapped at her, “You’re quite something, aren’t you? So tough, so long-lived. Planning to make it to a hundred?” The old woman would dismiss it, saying, “A hundred is nothing. Under the Emperor’s watchful eye, I’ll live to two hundred, three hundred!” But then her voice would drop, weary and listless: “I’ve lived long enough. There’s no point anymore. My son is gone, my grandson is gone. My daughter married and cut me off, hasn’t come to see me in years. I thought the hunchback would take good care of me, but he turned out heartless and just slipped away on his own, leaving me all alone. I can’t swallow that. If he doesn’t come back, I won’t die. I have to see him face to face and ask him why he just abandoned me. Even if I die, I want to die knowing why!”
