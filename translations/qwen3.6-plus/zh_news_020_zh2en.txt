Full Implementation of New Curriculum Standards for Compulsory Education

The Ministry of Education has announced that the new curriculum standards for compulsory education will be fully implemented across primary and secondary schools nationwide starting in the fall semester of 2026. Guided by core competencies, the new standards increase the proportion of science, labor, and art education. Notably, the starting grade for science classes has been adjusted from Grade 3 to Grade 1, while the share of class hours for Physical Education and Health has been raised to 10%–11%. Textbook compilation and teacher training programs are already fully underway and are expected to cover approximately 160 million primary and secondary school students.

The advancement of compulsory education reform also faces challenges such as achieving balanced regional development and strengthening the teaching workforce. Educational conditions in central and western regions and rural schools continue to improve, with teacher shortages in key subjects being addressed through initiatives like the Special Post Teacher Program and the Silver-Age Teaching Program. The digital education strategy initiative has promoted the co-creation and sharing of high-quality educational resources. The continued implementation of the "Double Reduction" policy has alleviated students' academic workloads and off-campus tutoring burdens. Meanwhile, education evaluation reform is exploring the establishment of a more scientific and diversified assessment system.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts note that these changes will have a profound impact on the sector's development landscape. In the long run, all stakeholders must strengthen coordination to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have created a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with the deepening of technological advancement and innovative models, related fields are expected to see faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society look forward to this progress with great anticipation.
