The museum building was quite large, stretching for twenty or thirty rooms. A gatekeeper sat outside the door, his cat-like head leaning against the wall, fast asleep. I poked my head in to look; not another soul was in sight. It was strange that the museum’s four doors could be left wide open with no one guarding them! And given how fond the Cat People were of stealing, it was even stranger! Not daring to disturb the sleeping gatekeeper, I ventured inside on my own. After passing through two empty rooms, I ran into my new friend. He was exceedingly cheerful, clean, lively, and polite, and I couldn’t help but take a great liking to him. His name was Catlovsky. I knew this was certainly not a common name in Cat Country; it had to be a foreign word. Fearing he might launch into a long string of words ending in “-lovsky,” I got straight to the point: I wanted to tour the antiquities and asked for his guidance. I figured he wouldn’t “-lovsky” the artifacts themselves; as long as he didn’t, I’d be fine.
