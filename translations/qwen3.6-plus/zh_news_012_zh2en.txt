Major Progress in Deep-Sea Exploration Technology

The China Ocean Mineral Resources Association announced that the Fendouzhe manned submersible has completed its 100th dive in the Mariana Trench of the Pacific Ocean, reaching a maximum depth of 10,909 meters. Meanwhile, domestically developed deep-sea equipment, including deep-sea drilling vessels and seabed observation networks, are also advancing steadily. China's technological capabilities for the exploration and development of deep-sea mineral resources have now entered the world's leading ranks. Scientists emphasize that deep-sea research holds irreplaceable scientific value for understanding Earth's evolution, climate change, and the origin of life.

Advances in deep-sea exploration technology have also provided new means for marine resource development and environmental protection. Deep-sea mineral resources primarily include polymetallic nodules, cobalt-rich crusts, and hydrothermal sulfides, which contain strategic metal elements such as nickel, cobalt, copper, and manganese. China holds multiple exclusive exploration contract areas in the international seabed area. Research on deep-sea ecological environments is advancing in parallel, with scientists using deep-sea submersibles to conduct long-term observations of unique ecosystems such as hydrothermal vents and cold seeps, leading to the discovery of numerous new species.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these developments will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. Continuous policy optimization and positive market feedback have created a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with the deepening advancement of technological iteration and model innovation, related fields are expected to experience faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society hold high expectations for this progress.
