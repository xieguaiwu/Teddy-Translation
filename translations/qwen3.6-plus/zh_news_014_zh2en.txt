World's Largest Offshore Wind Power Project Connected to the Grid

The world's largest offshore wind power project, constructed by Chinese enterprises, has achieved full-capacity grid connection in the waters off Yangjiang, Guangdong. With a total installed capacity of 6.5 gigawatts, the project is expected to generate approximately 26 billion kilowatt-hours of electricity annually, sufficient to meet the yearly power needs of around 9 million households. The project utilizes several models of domestically produced large-capacity wind turbines, which have helped reduce construction costs. As a vital component of clean energy, offshore wind power is emerging as a key pillar in China's energy structure transition.

The development of offshore wind projects has also driven exports in related equipment manufacturing and engineering technical services. Chinese-made wind turbines and installation vessels have been exported to multiple countries and regions, including Europe and Southeast Asia. Integrated development models combining offshore wind with marine ranching, seawater hydrogen production, and wave power generation are currently being explored. Breakthroughs in deep-sea floating wind technology have laid the foundation for harnessing wind resources in deeper and more distant waters. The rapid expansion of the offshore wind sector provides crucial support for China's goals of peaking carbon emissions and achieving carbon neutrality.

This trend has drawn widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts note that these developments will profoundly shape the future landscape of the sector. Continuous policy optimization and positive market responses have fostered a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, driven by ongoing technological upgrades and innovative business models, the sector is poised for faster and higher-quality development.
