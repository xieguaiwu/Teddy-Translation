"Ci, you love the sheep, but I love the ritual!"

The quote is from the *Ba Yi* chapter of *The Analects*: "Zigong wanted to do away with the xi-sheep for the Gao Shuo ceremony. The Master said, 'Ci, you love the sheep, but I love the ritual!'" According to the commentary by Zhu Xi of the Song Dynasty: the xi-sheep is a live sheep. The feudal lords, on the first day of each month (the first day of the lunar month), would report to the ancestral temple to administer government affairs, which was called Gao Shuo. Zigong (Duanmu Ci), seeing that the ruler of the State of Lu at the time had already abolished the Gao Shuo ritual, wanted to also do away with the sheep prepared for the ceremony; but Confucius thought that with the sheep, at least a semblance of the ritual could be preserved, hence his words.

(1) Ci originated from Yuefu, and Yuefu originated from Shi. The materials of the Major and Minor *Ya* among the Four Divisions of Poetry total one hundred and five. The elegance of the material is what makes the *Feng* beautiful, and what forms the *Song*. From Shi to Yuefu to Ci, one must aspire to the single word "ya" (elegance) in order to stand out and style oneself as a creator. Therefore, Zeng Duanbo selected ci and named the collection *Yuefu Yaci*, and Zhou Gongjin, who was good at writing ci, titled his hall "Zhiya". As a literary form, ci is meandering and gentle; if it is not woven with elegance, it will rarely avoid drifting with the waves and losing its proper course. (Preface to *Qunya Ciji*)
