Significant Progress in Deep-Sea Exploration Technology

The China Ocean Mineral Resources R&D Association announced that the Fendouzhe manned submersible completed its 100th dive mission in the Mariana Trench in the Pacific Ocean, reaching a maximum depth of 10,909 meters. Meanwhile, domestically developed deep-sea equipment projects, such as deep-sea drill ships and seafloor observation networks, are also steadily advancing. The technological reserves for the exploration and development of deep-sea mineral resources now rank among the world's best. Scientists state that deep-sea research holds irreplaceable scientific value for understanding Earth's evolution, climate change, and the origin of life.

Advances in deep-sea exploration technology have also provided new tools for the development of marine resources and the protection of the marine environment. Deep-sea mineral resources primarily include polymetallic nodules, cobalt-rich crusts, and hydrothermal sulfides, which contain strategic metal elements such as nickel, cobalt, copper, and manganese. China holds several exclusive exploration contract areas in the International Seabed Area. Research on deep-sea ecological environments is also progressing simultaneously; scientists have utilized deep-sea probes to conduct long-term observations of unique ecosystems such as hydrothermal vents and cold seeps, discovering numerous new species.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these changes will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and collaboration to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have created a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, as technological iteration and model innovation continue to advance, related fields are expected to experience faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society hold high expectations for this.
