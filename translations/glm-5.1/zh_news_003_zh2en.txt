New Quantum Computer Breaks the 1,000-Qubit Barrier

Pan Jianwei's team at the University of Science and Technology of China has announced the successful development of a superconducting quantum computer with over 1,000 qubits. On specific computational tasks, this quantum computer has achieved computing speeds unmatched by classical supercomputers, reaching a new milestone in quantum error correction efficiency. The research team stated that this breakthrough makes it possible to apply quantum computers in practical scenarios such as financial modeling and drug molecular simulation. The related findings have been published in the journal *Nature*.

The global race to develop quantum computers is accelerating. Countries and regions such as the United States, the European Union, and Japan have successively launched large-scale investment plans for quantum technology. China has made significant progress across multiple technological routes, including superconducting quantum computing, photonic quantum computing, and ion traps. Experts note that the commercial application of quantum computing still needs to overcome key technical challenges such as qubit coherence time, error correction efficiency, and system integration. However, breakthroughs in basic research are steadily bridging the gap from the laboratory to industrialization.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these developments will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and collaboration to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with the deepening of technological iteration and model innovation, related fields are expected to witness faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society are highly anticipating this.
