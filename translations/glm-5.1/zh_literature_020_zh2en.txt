Shazaohua, who had been missing for years, returned from who knows where. She was well-preserved, looking to be only in her thirties. She came to the tower to see her mother, but her mother's reaction was very indifferent. In the days that followed, she and Sima Liang enacted a very classical life-and-death romance. She produced a glass marble, claiming it was a love token Sima Liang had given her. She then produced a large mirror, claiming it was a love token she had given him. She claimed she had preserved her virginity for Sima Liang to this day. Sima Liang, staying in the top-floor presidential suite of the Osmanthus Building, was heavy-hearted upon this return, and had no mood to renew his old romance with Shazaohua. Shazaohua, however, followed him like a pesky shadow, annoying Sima Liang so much that he bared his teeth, stomped his feet, jumped in the air, and roared like thunder: "My good cousin, what exactly do you want? I offer you money, you refuse; I offer you clothes, you refuse; I offer you jewelry, you refuse. What is it you want?!" Sima Liang shook off Shazaohua's hand tugging at the corner of his jacket and plopped down on the sofa in angry helplessness. His raised foot kicked over a glass water pitcher with a thin neck and a big belly; water flowed across the table, soaking the carpet, and a dozen or so purplish-red roses hung messily over the edge of the table. Wearing a black dress as thin as cicada wings, Shazaohua knelt beside Sima Liang, clinging to him, her pitch-black eyes staring fixedly at his face, leaving him no choice but to look her in the eye. Her head was delicate, her neck slender and smooth, with only a few fine wrinkles. Sima Liang, experienced with women, knew that the neck was a woman's telltale growth ring, impossible to conceal. The neck of a fifty-year-old woman, if it didn't resemble a swollen section of large intestine, looked like a piece of rotten dead wood. It was rare to see a smooth, upright neck like Shazaohua's on a woman in her fifties; who knew how she maintained it. Sima Liang's gaze traced down her neck to her two deep shoulder hollows and the hazy breasts beneath her dress. From any angle, she didn't look like a woman in her fifties; she was a flower refrigerated for half a century. A bottle of osmanthus wine buried beneath a pomegranate tree for half a century. The ice-cold flower awaited plucking, the viscous wine awaited quaffing. Sima Liang extended a finger and poked Shazaohua's bare knee. She let out a moan, her face flushing crimson like an evening glow. Like a hero fearless of death, she threw herself into Sima Liang's arms. Her clinging arms wrapped around his neck, and her hot bosom pressed tightly against his face, rubbing back and forth until it squeezed oil from his nose and sour tears from his eyes. Shazaohua said, "Brother Maliang, I've waited thirty years for you." Sima Liang replied, "Zaohua, stop this nonsense. Waited thirty years for me—what a heavy sin to lay at my door." Shazaohua said, "I am a virgin." Sima Liang retorted, "A female thief, and a virgin at that! If you're a virgin, I'll jump right off this building!" Shazaohua cried aggrievedly, mumbling under her breath. Her mumbling turned into anger as she jumped up, gave a bounce, and dropped her dress to her feet like a snake shedding its skin. Lying flat on her back on the carpet, she shrieked: "Sima Liang, try it and see for yourself! If I'm not a virgin, I'll jump off the building!"
