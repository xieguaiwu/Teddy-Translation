World's Largest Offshore Wind Farm Connected to Grid

The world's largest offshore wind project, built by Chinese companies, has achieved full-capacity grid connection in the waters off Yangjiang, Guangdong. With a total installed capacity of 6.5 GW and an annual power generation of approximately 26 billion kWh, the project can meet the annual electricity demand of around 9 million households. The project utilized several models of domestically produced large-capacity wind turbines, which reduced construction costs. As a crucial component of clean energy, offshore wind power is becoming a key pillar in the transformation of China's energy structure.

The construction of offshore wind projects has also driven the export of related equipment manufacturing and engineering technical services. Chinese-made wind turbines and installation vessels have been exported to multiple countries and regions, including Europe and Southeast Asia. Integrated development models combining offshore wind power with marine ranching, hydrogen production from seawater, and wave power generation are also being explored. Breakthroughs in deep-sea floating wind power technology have laid the foundation for developing deeper and more distant offshore wind energy resources. The rapid development of the offshore wind industry provides crucial support for China to achieve its carbon peak and carbon neutrality goals.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these changes will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, as technological iteration and model innovation advance further, related fields are expected to see more rapid and high-quality development. All sectors of society hold high expectations for this.
