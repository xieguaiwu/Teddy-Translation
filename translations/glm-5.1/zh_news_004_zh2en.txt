Total Number of New Energy Vehicles in China Exceeds 40 Million

The latest data from the Ministry of Public Security shows that as of the end of May 2026, the total number of new energy vehicles (NEVs) in China reached 41.5 million, accounting for 12.7% of the total vehicle fleet. In the first five months of 2026, newly registered NEVs reached 4.2 million, a year-on-year increase of 45%. In terms of charging infrastructure, the total number of chargers nationwide reached 12 million, optimizing the vehicle-to-charger ratio to 3.5:1. Industry insiders believe that with advancements in battery technology and the improvement of charging networks, the market penetration rate of NEVs will exceed 30% in 2027.

The rapid development of the NEV industry has also driven the coordinated progress of upstream and downstream industry chains. The energy density of power batteries continues to increase, and the costs of lithium iron phosphate and ternary lithium batteries have dropped by approximately 60% and 50%, respectively. Next-generation battery technologies, such as solid-state and sodium-ion batteries, are also under accelerated research and development. The integrated application of intelligent connected technologies has significantly enhanced the user experience of NEVs in areas such as autonomous driving and smart cabins. The deployment of charging and battery-swapping infrastructure is extending into highway service areas, townships, and communities.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these changes will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with the deepening advancement of technological iterations and business model innovations, related fields are expected to experience even faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society hold high expectations for this.
