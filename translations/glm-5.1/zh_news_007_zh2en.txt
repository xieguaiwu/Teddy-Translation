Urban Renewal Initiative Drives Renovation of Old Residential Communities

According to the latest report from the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, renovation projects have been launched in a cumulative total of 280,000 old urban residential communities nationwide, benefiting over 65 million households. The 2026 plan aims to renovate 53,000 old communities, with a focus on installing elevators, upgrading water, electricity, gas, and heating networks, and adding parking spaces and charging stations. Various regions are also exploring new models to introduce social capital for participation in renovation and operation. Experts point out that urban renewal is not only a livelihood project but also an important avenue for boosting domestic demand and promoting economic growth.

During the renovation of old communities, various regions are also exploring the establishment of long-term management mechanisms. Renovated communities ensure the sustainability of these improvements by introducing professional property management, establishing homeowners' associations, and setting up maintenance funds. The integration of smart community development with the renovation of old communities has driven the widespread adoption of facilities such as smart access control, smart parking, and smart waste sorting. Resident satisfaction surveys indicate that living comfort in renovated communities has increased by an average of over 40%.

This trend has sparked widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts point out that these changes will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous cycle. In the coming years, with the deepening advancement of technological iteration and model innovation, related fields are expected to experience more rapid and high-quality development. All sectors of society hold great expectations for this.
