Grocer Zhang took Zhu Xingyun’s sudden disappearance in stride. She figured he wouldn’t die; in these times, men vanishing out of the blue was a common occurrence. At first, she had been anxious for a spell, asking people everywhere for news, and even making a special trip to the countryside where her husband had gone. But after coming up empty-handed, she stopped wasting her energy. She figured her husband must be unable to return home, otherwise he would have come back long ago. It was useless searching the whole world for him. The burden of running the household had fallen squarely on her shoulders, which was enough to make her stagger; who would have expected that Wang Jintang’s wife would come knocking on her door, demanding that Zhu Xingyun produce the man? She claimed that since Zhu Xingyun had taken Wang Jintang away, he was responsible for bringing him back. Grocer Zhang wasn’t having any of it. She gave the old woman a thorough scolding and chased her out the door. Yet, to her surprise, the old woman started coming to the grocery store every day after that. She didn’t come inside; she just stood outside shivering in the cold wind, saying to anyone who passed by: "Did you know? My hunchback went with Zhu Xingyun to haul sticky bean buns for the grocery store, and he still hasn't come back. I came here to ask them for my man, and this woman actually cursed me out. Tell me, is that any way to behave? What right does she have to curse at me the moment she opens her mouth?" At first, Grocer Zhang sent her two children out to chase the old woman away, but when they found it impossible to get rid of her, she took matters into her own hands, brandishing a fire poker and threatening to crack her over the head. But the old woman had seen her share of the world and was completely unfazed. Grocer Zhang also felt that the old woman had been left all alone because of her own family’s affairs, so she simply let her stay, declaring, "Just consider that I picked up an old dog." Grocer Zhang even brazenly pushed a wheelbarrow over to Wang Jintang’s house, moving over the usable items one by one. She assured the old woman that the house was empty now and the things would be stolen by thieves if left there; keeping them at the grocery store was just for safekeeping, and they would be returned intact once Wang Jintang came back. The old woman thought this made sense and let her be. Grocer Zhang, however, was quite shrewd. She quietly sold off the old woman’s belongings, thinking to herself, "I can’t just feed you for nothing. If your hunchback doesn’t come back for eight or ten years, am I supposed to wait on you forever?" In her mind, this befuddled, trembling old woman wouldn’t live much longer anyway. Who could have expected that over the past two years, she would cling to life so tenaciously? She was always complaining of dizziness and weakness, yet she never took a single fall when walking alone. Though she didn’t eat much, she never missed a single meal, and the chopsticks she held never once slipped from her hands. Grocer Zhang snapped at her more than once: "You’re really something, aren’t you? Tough as nails. Aiming for a hundred, are you?" The old woman dismissed this: "A hundred is nothing. Right under the Emperor’s nose, I’ll live to be two or three hundred!" Finally, she would lower her voice, speaking with a listless weakness: "I’ve lived long enough. There’s no point anymore. My son is gone, and my grandson is gone. My daughter married and washed her hands of me; she hasn’t come to see me in years. I thought my hunchback would treat me well, but it turns out he’s just as heartless, sneaking off alone and leaving me by myself. I just can’t swallow this injustice. If he doesn’t come back, I refuse to die. I have to look him in the eye and ask him why he could just abandon me like that. Even if I die, I’m going to die knowing why!"
