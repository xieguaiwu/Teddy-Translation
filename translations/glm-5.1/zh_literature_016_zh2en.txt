Amidst the rhythmic thudding of Zijing chopping the filling, Huangmao leaned his tired body against the wall and began telling the old woman all sorts of stories. "Blind Mother, have you ever heard this? There was a woman in Wanggezhuang who went to fetch water early in the morning, and suddenly saw a red lotus flower as big as a woven rush cushion in the well. The red lotus was holding a fair, chubby baby. The woman was bewitched and lost her mind; she plunged headfirst into the well and drowned—" "The lotus baby was a soul-reaping ghost in disguise," the old woman said. "—One day, it was raining heavily, and eight masons ran into a ruined temple to take shelter. The thunder and lightning were continuous, like an unbroken sheet, and fireballs rolled back and forth in front of the temple door. The people inside were scared out of their wits. One of them said, 'Among the eight of us, someone must have done something against their conscience. We can't let one rat dropping ruin a whole pot of porridge. Whoever is guilty should go out.' But who was willing to go out? So they pushed and shoved each other, becoming a tangled mess. Another person said, 'How about this: everyone take off your bamboo hats and throw them out the temple door. Whoever's hat is blown away by the wind will go out to die.' One person braced himself and opened the temple door; the wind and rain whooshed in. Everyone took turns throwing their hats out, but every thrown hat was blown right back. Seven hats were thrown, and all seven were blown back. Only one person was left. He tremblingly picked up his hat and threw it out, and a gust of evil wind swept the hat away. The seven men said, 'It's you, go on out.' How could he be willing to go? The seven men didn't give him a choice; they lifted him up and threw him out—" "What happened then? Was this man struck by lightning?" "—Blind Mother, listen to me. After that man was thrown out, he knelt on the ground. He kowtowed as fast as pounding garlic, praying, 'Heavens, Heavens, you must not wrong a good person!' Just as he was praying, he heard a loud crash behind him. The entire ruined temple collapsed. The four walls fell inward, the roof pressed down, and not one of the seven men escaped. They were wrapped into a giant human-filled dumpling—" "Oh my, how could such a thing happen!" the old woman exclaimed repeatedly. The displeasure brought on by the gloomy weather completely vanished. Just as she was enthusiastically listening to Huangmao tell the next story, Zijing brought out the steaming dumplings. The old woman's interest was still high, and she made Huangmao promise to continue telling her stories after he ate. Zijing brought over a bowl of jellyfish and a dish of century eggs, pouted at Huangmao, and said, "There's a bottle of wine in the back window hole. Have a couple of sips, it'll relieve your fatigue." The old woman said, "Have a little, you've worked hard all day." Huangmao took the wine, pried the cap off with his teeth, and took three big gulps. The alcohol hit him quickly, and his face flushed with the vibrant red of peach blossoms. Zijing snatched the bottle from his hand, gulped down a mouthful, and tears instantly filled her eyes. Huangmao's face floated in the curling white steam, like a phantom drifting near and far.

Several people had already crawled through the tunnel into the hole. Lao Zhang was the first. He crawled in front, holding the lamp above the water to illuminate the way. But he could not see anything, only that expanse of murky water. The water constantly slapped his face, hurting him. He raised his head to avoid submerging in the water, and his body was almost trapped by the sludge. He used great effort to crawl forward a few steps. The water ahead rushed over and pushed him back a step. In a moment of carelessness, his hand loosened, and the lamp fell into the water and went out. His head submerged in the water, his vision went black, and a ringing sounded in his ears.

"How can I forget the past?" he forced out, his face flushed. "The most important thing is that you have the husband you chose, and I have my Zhijun."

"The writing reflects the man"—this saying cannot always be trusted. Writing is sometimes expression, an opening up; sometimes it is a cover-up, an evasion. Behind tear-jerking words, there may well be concealment. I myself have had such experiences; often, when yearning to express myself, I have instead concealed much, and I knew in my heart that what was hidden might be more important than what was expressed. Why is this? Why hide when you know in your heart? Why evade when you know it is important?

"They want to eat me, and you alone could do nothing about it; but why must you join them? People who eat others are capable of anything; they will eat me, and they will eat you, and within their own gang, they will eat each other. But if you just change course, if you change immediately, then everyone will be at peace. Even though it has always been this way, we can still choose to treat each other better today, can't we! Big brother, I believe you can say no. The day before yesterday, when the tenant farmers asked for a rent reduction, you said no."

Zijin's way of speaking made Feng Zicun feel quite ill at ease. He rarely spoke, and even when he occasionally uttered a word or two, he was evasive, as if deliberately keeping his thoughts impenetrable.

"You don't need to comfort me, I am not afraid of death. Life is meaningless, I might as well die! Dying would also save the country some rations," Fourth Uncle said with a smile.
