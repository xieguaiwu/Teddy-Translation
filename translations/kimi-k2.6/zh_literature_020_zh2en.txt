The long-missing Shazao Hua returned from some unknown place. She had taken excellent care of herself and looked only about thirty-something. She came to see her mother before the pagoda, but her mother's reaction was very indifferent. In the days that followed, she and Sima Liang carried on a very classical, life-and-death romance. She took out a glass ball, saying it was the love token Sima Liang had given her. Then she took out a large mirror, saying it was the love token she had given him. She said she had preserved her virginity for Sima Liang to this day. Sima Liang, who was staying in the presidential suite on the top floor of the Osmanthus Building, had returned this time with a heart full of worries and was in no mood to rekindle old passions with Shazao Hua. But Shazao Hua followed him closely like a shadow, annoying Sima Liang so much that he bared his teeth, stamped his feet, jumped up and down, and roared like thunder: "My dear cousin, what on earth do you want? I give you money, you don't want it; I give you clothes, you don't want them; I give you jewelry, you don't want it—what do you want?!" Sima Liang shook off Shazao Hua's hand that was clutching the corner of his garment, and sat down heavily on the sofa in an angry, helpless manner. His raised foot kicked over a large-bellied, narrow-necked glass water bottle. Water flowed across the table, soaking the carpet, and more than ten purplish-red roses hung in disarray over the edge of the table. Shazao Hua was wearing a black dress as thin as cicada wings. She knelt close beside Sima Liang, clinging to him, her jet-black eyes staring straight at his face, forcing him to look her in the eye. Her head was delicate, her neck slender and smooth, with only a few fine wrinkles. Sima Liang, who was experienced with women, knew that the neck was a woman's inescapable annual ring: the neck of a fifty-year-old woman was either like a swollen section of intestine or a rotting piece of dead wood. It was rare to find a neck as smooth and erect as Shazao Hua's at fifty-something; he didn't know how she maintained it. Sima Liang followed her neck downward with his eyes, seeing her two deep shoulder hollows and the hazy outline of her breasts beneath the dress. No matter which part of her he looked at, she didn't resemble a woman in her fifties. She was a flower kept refrigerated for half a century. She was a bottle of osmanthus wine buried beneath a pomegranate tree for half a century. An icy flower waiting to be plucked; a thick wine waiting to be drunk. Sima Liang extended a finger and poked Shazao Hua's exposed knee. She moaned, her face flooding with color like a sunset glow. Like a hero who fears neither life nor death, she threw herself into Sima Liang's arms. Her clinging arms wrapped around his neck; her burning-hot bosom pressed against his face, rubbing back and forth, chafing oil from his nose and drawing stinging tears from his eyes. Shazao Hua said, "Brother Maliang, I've waited for you thirty years." Sima Liang said, "Zaohua, cut the crap. Waiting for me thirty years—what a crime to pin on my head." Shazao Hua said, "I'm a virgin." Sima Liang said, "A female thief, and a virgin? If you're a virgin, I'll jump off this building!" Weeping with grievance, Shazao Hua muttered to herself; her muttering turned to fury, and she jumped up, bounced once, and like a snake shedding its skin, let her dress fall to her feet. Lying on her back on the carpet, she shouted, "Sima Liang, you try me out! If I'm not a virgin, I'll jump off the building!"
