China Space Station Launches Fifth Batch of Scientific Experiment Projects

China Manned Space Engineering Office announced that the Tiangong space station has officially launched its fifth batch of scientific experiment projects. This batch comprises 32 projects covering space life sciences, microgravity physics, space materials science, astronomical observation, and other fields, with international cooperation projects accounting for 40 percent. Research teams from 17 countries will conduct experiments aboard China's space station. To date, the Tiangong space station has completed more than 200 scientific experiments, producing a number of internationally influential research findings.

The international cooperation model of China's space station has received positive recognition from the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs. Through joint experiments, astronaut exchanges, data sharing, and other forms, China's space station is becoming a new platform for global space science research cooperation. The station's scalable design also provides a technology verification platform for future deep-space exploration missions. Research and development of technologies related to manned lunar missions is also advancing in parallel, with the goal of sending Chinese astronauts to the moon expected to be achieved within this decade.
