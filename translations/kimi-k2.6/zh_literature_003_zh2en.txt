Grocer Zhang took Zhu Xingyun's sudden disappearance in stride. She figured he wouldn't die—men suddenly going missing was common enough in these times. At first she had been anxious for a while, asking everyone to look into it, even going to the countryside where her husband had gone; after coming up empty, she stopped troubling herself. She figured her husband surely couldn't return home, or he would have come back long ago. There was no use searching the whole world for him. The burden of supporting the family falling on her shoulders alone was already enough to make her stagger, but then Wang Jintang's wife came knocking, insisting that Zhu Xingyun produce her husband. She said he had taken Wang Jintang away, so he should be the one to bring him back. Grocer Zhang wouldn't stand for that. She cursed the old woman out and chased her from the door. But after that, the old lady came to the grocery shop every single day. She wouldn't enter, just shivering in the cold wind, stopping everyone to say, "Do you know? My hunchback went off with Zhu Xingyun to deliver sticky bean buns for the grocery shop, and he hasn't come back yet. I came to ask them for my man, and this woman cursed me. Tell me, is she reasonable or not? What gives her the right to open her mouth and curse?" At first, Grocer Zhang sent her son and daughter out to drive her away, but when they couldn't get rid of her, she went out herself, waving a fire poker and threatening to crack the old lady over the head. The old woman had seen the world; she wasn't intimidated by that. Grocer Zhang also felt that because of her own family's affairs, the old woman had been left alone and helpless, so she simply let her stay, claiming, "Consider it as if I picked up an old stray dog." Grocer Zhang even boldly pushed a wheelbarrow over to Wang Jintang's house and carted over everything usable, assuring the old woman that the house was empty now and the things inside would be stolen by thieves; storing them at the grocery shop was just temporary, and they would be returned intact when Wang Jintang came back. The old woman thought that made sense and let her do as she pleased. Grocer Zhang was quite calculating. She quietly sold off the old woman's belongings, thinking: I can't feed you for nothing. If your hunchback doesn't come back for ten or eight years, am I supposed to keep serving you like this? By her reckoning, this muddle-headed, trembling old woman wouldn't live much longer anyway. Who would have thought that these past two years she had been remarkably tenacious? She was always complaining of dizziness and weakness, yet she had never fallen once when walking alone. Though her appetite was small, she never missed a meal, and the chopsticks she picked up never slipped from her hands. More than once, Grocer Zhang snapped at her, "You're something else, aren't you? Good at hanging on, good at living—are you planning to reach a hundred?" The old woman replied dismissively, "What's a hundred? Under the emperor's very eyes, I must live to two hundred, three hundred!" Then she lowered her voice and said listlessly, "I've lived enough; it's meaningless. My sons are gone, my grandsons are gone. My daughter married and ignores me, hasn't come back to see me in years. I thought the hunchback would treat me well, but who knew he was heartless too, slipping off alone, leaving me behind. I can't swallow this anger. If he doesn't come back, I won't die. I have to see him and ask him: why did you just wash your hands of me? If I must die, I'll die knowing the truth!"
