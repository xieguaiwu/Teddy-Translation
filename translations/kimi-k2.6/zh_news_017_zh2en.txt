China's Cross-Border E-Commerce Trade Hits New Record

Data released by the Ministry of Commerce shows that in 2025, China's cross-border e-commerce imports and exports reached 2.8 trillion yuan, up 32% year on year. Exports were dominated by clothing and footwear, electronic products, and household goods, while imports focused on beauty and skincare products, maternal and infant products, and food and health supplements. Growth in emerging markets such as Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America was particularly notable. The number of overseas warehouses exceeded 2,500, covering major consumer markets worldwide. Cross-border e-commerce is becoming an important new engine for foreign trade growth.

The rapid development of cross-border e-commerce has also benefited from continuous improvements in logistics infrastructure and the refinement of international payment systems. Companies such as Cainiao Network and JD Logistics have established efficient sorting and delivery networks overseas, reducing cross-border parcel delivery times from two weeks to three to five days. Cross-border payment platforms support multi-currency settlement and localized payment methods. The number of comprehensive pilot zones for cross-border e-commerce has expanded to 165, producing a batch of institutional innovations in customs clearance, taxation, and settlement that can be replicated and promoted.

This trend has triggered widespread attention and discussion. Industry experts note that these changes will have a profound impact on the industry's development landscape. In the long run, all parties need to strengthen coordination and cooperation to jointly address challenges and seize opportunities. The continuous optimization of policies and positive market feedback have formed a virtuous interaction. In the coming years, as technological iteration and model innovation advance further, related fields are expected to embrace even faster and higher-quality development. All sectors of society are eagerly anticipating this.
