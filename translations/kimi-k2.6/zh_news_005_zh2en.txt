Glacial Melting Accelerates in Mount Everest Region

The latest monitoring report released by the Institute of Tibetan Plateau Research at the Chinese Academy of Sciences shows that glaciers in the Mount Everest region have melted approximately 2.5 times faster over the past decade than in the previous three decades. By analyzing satellite remote sensing data and ground observation station records, researchers found that in 2025, the total mass loss of glaciers in the Everest region reached a record 1.2 billion tons. Global warming is the primary cause of the accelerated glacial melting. This trend will affect water supplies for several major rivers in Asia, impacting hundreds of millions of people.

The accelerating effects of glacial melting have already had a direct impact on surrounding ecosystems and local residents' lives. The expansion of lakes formed by glacial meltwater has increased the risk of outburst floods. Downstream river runoff has increased in the short term but faces the threat of long-term decline. Researchers have called for strengthening glacial monitoring networks and formulating regional water resource management plans that adapt to climate change. Changes in the Everest region's glaciers also serve as a stark warning signal of global climate warming.
