The Baker's First Morning

At four in the morning, the bakery was the warmest place in the city. Marco arrived before anyone else, as he had done for thirty-two years, and switched on the ovens. The old brick walls absorbed the heat and released it slowly, creating a cocoon of warmth against the cold November dark. He mixed the dough by hand, as his father had taught him, feeling the texture change as the ingredients came together. The first batch of bread went into the oven at five. By six, the smell would drift out into the street, pulling people from their sleep.

By six o'clock, the first customers arrived, drawn by the smell of fresh bread and the warm glow of the bakery windows. Marco knew most of them by name. There was Mrs. Chen, who always bought a pain de campagne and a croissant; Mr. Tanaka, who preferred the sourdough; and the young woman from the apartment upstairs who came for a baguette still warm from the oven. He knew their stories, too, the fragments they shared while he wrapped their purchases. Mrs. Chen's son was in medical school. Mr. Tanaka had recently retired. The young woman was learning to bake herself, though she did not say so. Marco listened and nodded and handed them their bread. It was enough.
