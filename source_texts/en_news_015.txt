Quantum Internet Achieves First Intercontinental Connection

Researchers successfully demonstrated the first intercontinental quantum internet connection, transmitting quantum-encrypted data between ground stations in Beijing and Vienna via a quantum satellite. The achievement represents a major step toward building a global quantum communication network that would be immune to eavesdropping. The system maintained quantum entanglement over a distance of 7,600 kilometers.

The quantum satellite, named Micius-2 after the ancient Chinese philosopher, serves as a trusted relay node that distributes entangled photon pairs to ground stations. The intercontinental demonstration builds on earlier experiments that achieved quantum key distribution between satellites and ground stations over shorter distances. The technology has immediate applications in secure communications for government, financial, and military purposes. The research team plans to launch a constellation of quantum satellites within five years, providing continuous global coverage.
