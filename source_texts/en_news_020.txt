Last Passenger Pigeon Genome Successfully Sequenced

In a bittersweet milestone for conservation biology, scientists have successfully sequenced the complete genome of the passenger pigeon, a species that went extinct in 1914. The genome, extracted from museum specimens, provides insights into the genetic diversity of the once-abundant bird and informs de-extinction efforts. A research consortium is already exploring the feasibility of bringing the species back through genetic engineering.

The passenger pigeon genome project was led by researchers at the University of California Santa Cruz in collaboration with the Revive and Restore foundation. The genome reveals that passenger pigeons had relatively low genetic diversity compared to living bird species, suggesting that their population underwent periodic bottlenecks even before human arrival. The de-extinction effort would involve editing the genome of the band-tailed pigeon, the passenger pigeon's closest living relative, to express passenger pigeon traits. Critics question whether de-extinction resources would be better spent on preventing extinction of currently threatened species.
