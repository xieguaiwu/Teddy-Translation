The Library of Forgotten Books

In a basement beneath the city's main library, there existed a collection of books that no one had borrowed in over fifty years. Dust thickened on their spines like sediment. The oldest was a travelogue from 1887, describing a journey through a country that no longer existed. There were obsolete textbooks, out-of-print novels, political manifestos whose causes had been won or lost long ago. The librarian in charge of this collection, a quiet woman named Helen, considered herself their caretaker. She did not read them often. But she liked knowing they were there, waiting for someone who might one day come looking.

One day, a young man came down to the basement, looking for a book that had been out of print since 1923. Helen found it for him, in a section she had not visited in years. He was a graduate student, he explained, writing a dissertation on forgotten travel writers of the nineteenth century. She watched him as he opened the book, his fingers careful on the brittle pages. For a moment, she saw herself, thirty years ago, discovering the basement for the first time. She did not tell him that she had read every book in this collection, one by one, over the course of her career.
