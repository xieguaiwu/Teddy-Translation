The Photograph

In the back of a drawer, beneath a collection of old receipts and unused keys, he found a photograph of a woman he did not recognize. She was standing on a beach, squinting into the sun, her hair blown across her face. On the back, in handwriting he recognized as his own, was written: Brighton, 1983. He had no memory of this. He had never been to Brighton, as far as he knew. He studied the woman's face, trying to find something familiar in the curve of her smile. Nothing. He put the photograph back in the drawer and closed it.

Years later, he learned the truth. The woman in the photograph was his mother, before she became his mother. She had taken a trip to Brighton with friends, the summer before she married. The photograph had been taken by her father, his grandfather, whose handwriting he now recognized. He had found the photograph in a drawer in his mother's house after she died, and the shock of seeing his own handwriting on the back had created the false memory. He looked at the photograph again, seeing it differently now. That was his mother, young and free, standing on a beach, her whole life ahead of her.
