The Letter

She found the letter in a book she had borrowed from the library, pressed between pages one hundred and twelve and one hundred and thirteen. The paper was thin and yellowed, the handwriting elegant and unfamiliar. It began, My dearest Thomas, and ended, Yours always, Eleanor. She read it three times, standing there in the quiet corner of the reading room. A love letter, never sent, hidden in a novel about lost love. She looked around, as if expecting someone to claim it. The librarian was shelving books near the window, oblivious. She slipped the letter into her pocket and checked out the book.

She took the book home and placed the letter on her desk. That evening, instead of reading, she found herself composing a response, though she knew she would never send it. Dear Eleanor, she wrote, I found your letter today. I do not know who you are or what became of you and Thomas. But I want you to know that your words reached someone, decades late but not too late. She stopped writing. The letter was not hers to answer. She folded it carefully and placed it back in the book, returning it to the library the next day. She left the letter where she had found it, waiting for the next reader.
