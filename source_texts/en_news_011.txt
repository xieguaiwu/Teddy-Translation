Global Internet Access Reaches 75% of World Population

The International Telecommunication Union reported that 75% of the global population now has access to the internet, representing approximately 6.2 billion people. The milestone was driven by the expansion of mobile broadband networks in South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa. Despite progress, a digital divide persists, with urban areas enjoying significantly higher connection speeds and lower costs than rural regions.

Mobile broadband networks, particularly in India and Nigeria, have been the primary drivers of increased connectivity, with affordable smartphones and low-cost data plans bringing hundreds of millions of people online for the first time. However, the report notes that connection quality varies enormously, with average download speeds in the fastest countries exceeding 200 Mbps compared to less than 10 Mbps in the slowest. The ITU has called for targeted investments in backbone infrastructure in underserved regions.

The development represents a significant shift in the global policy landscape. Experts have noted that the pace of change has accelerated considerably in recent years, driven by technological innovation, shifting public expectations, and the growing urgency of addressing long-standing challenges. International cooperation has played a crucial role, with multiple stakeholders contributing expertise and resources. While obstacles remain, the trajectory suggests continued progress in the years ahead. Observers will be watching closely to see how these developments unfold and what new challenges emerge as the landscape continues to evolve. The implications for various sectors are far-reaching, and adaptation will be necessary across the board.
