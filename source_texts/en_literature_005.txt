The Piano Tuner

He had been tuning pianos for forty-seven years and had never learned to play one. His hands knew the tension of strings, the feel of hammers, the mathematics of harmonics. But melody escaped him. He traveled from house to house, factory to school, listening to other people's music take shape. Sometimes, after finishing a job, he would sit in the quiet room and press a single key, just to hear it ring true. That was enough. In the evening, he returned to his small apartment, where no piano waited. He did not feel the lack.

One day, a young woman came to his apartment and asked him to teach her. She was a pianist, she explained, but she had lost something, a quality in her playing that she could not name. He laughed and told her he could not even play a scale. But she insisted. So he agreed, on the condition that she would not expect him to make music. They met once a week. He showed her how to feel the hammers, how to listen to the strings, how to understand the mechanics of sound. She listened carefully, and slowly, her playing changed. It became more patient, more deliberate. After a year, she played a concert and dedicated it to him.
