The Watercarrier

In the village at the edge of the desert, the watercarrier made his journey twice a day. He walked six miles to the well, filled two large containers, and carried them back on a yoke across his shoulders. The water was for the old ones who could no longer make the journey themselves. He was seventy-one years old and had been doing this since he was twelve. When asked why he did not let the younger generation take over, he smiled and said the water would not taste the same. It was not a logical answer, but the villagers understood.

One day, the well ran dry. The old watercarrier made the journey and found nothing but damp sand at the bottom. He stood there for a long time, looking at the empty well. Then he walked back to the village and told the elders. A meeting was called. The young ones said they would drill a new well, deeper than the old one. But the watercarrier knew that some things could not be replaced. The water from a new well would not have the same taste, the same memory. That evening, he sat on his doorstep and looked at the desert, wondering what he would do with his days now.
