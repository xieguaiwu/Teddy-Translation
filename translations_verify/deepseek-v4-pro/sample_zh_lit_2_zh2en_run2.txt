The train hurtled through the darkness, lights outside the window streaking past like shooting stars. The carriage was thick with the smells of instant noodles and cheap perfume. The old man opposite had fallen asleep, his head resting against the glass, mouth slightly agape. He glanced at his watch: 3:17 a.m. Four more hours until the station. He remembered the bag of oranges his mother had pressed into his hands when he left—still lying on the luggage rack now.
